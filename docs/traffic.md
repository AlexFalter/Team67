# Traffic

Traffic runs in the existing Colyseus Race room and the existing Three.js loop. No new dependencies or city rebuild are needed. The saved network contains three independent one-way tracks (95 nodes, 92 segments), with one entrance and exit per track. HB traffic uses the left uphill curb lane; cars from the road under the Polybahn carriage bridge use the right uphill curb lane. Traffic from the southern river road turns toward the road under Polybahn. Tracks have no shared nodes or branching junctions, and cars never switch routes. The uphill tracks leave the tram corridor open. Rooms open with cars already placed along its lanes. The old single scripted NPC traffic car is disabled in Race rooms (story limousine, shuttles, bikes and rail vehicles still use their existing systems).

## Live development

Run `npm run dev`. Create a room with **Dev mode** enabled. As host, open **Traffic editor** at the top left. Cars simulate in the lobby without starting a round. Opening the editor releases pointer lock and lets you orbit the current map; closing it returns to gameplay. **Focus roads** frames the network; a live counter shows current cars and the target. Other clients in the room see the same authoritative traffic, including live edits.

Alternatively, open `http://localhost:5173/?trafficEditor=1` or click **Inspect map**. The development editor opens automatically and creates a live development room as soon as the map loads, including after a page reload. **Disconnect simulation** keeps your road draft and lets you enter another room code; **Create / join test room** reconnects or joins that room for observation. If the server restarts, the editor clears the stale room and rendered cars so reconnection works without reopening the editor. Use the displayed room code in a second game tab, then **Start round** from the editor to test alongside a player. Only the development room's host can change traffic. Export before switching inspect/play modes: leaving the last client destroys that room.

- **Draw roads** (default): hold the left mouse button and draw roughly along a street, then release. The green preview snaps across the asphalt width to its center and samples the road height. Nearby sidewalks are tolerated up to 4 m; strokes stop at gaps instead of connecting across buildings. Broad intersections keep your drawn direction. New strokes add bidirectional roads with one lane each way, smooth connectors and automatic spawn/despawn endpoints. Nearby nodes join; landing on an existing segment splits it into a T junction. Crossings join when there is room for road segments at least 15 m long and their heights agree; bridges stay separate. Draw junctions deliberately and inspect the yellow lane/turn overlay.
- **Undo last stroke** restores the previous network. **Select roads by clicking** toggles orange road segments; **Delete selected roads** removes them together, and **Undo deletion** restores them. No bounding-box workflow is required.
- **Add node**: click a street surface. The editor stores its world position, including elevation.
- **Select**: click a node (or use the node dropdown); drag to orbit. Right drag pans; the wheel zooms.
- **Move selected**: click its new location. **Delete node** also removes its connected roads.
- **Connect selected → clicked**: select the first node, then click the second. Repeated clicks extend a road chain. Nodes must be at least 15 metres apart.
- Select a road in the dropdown to change one-way direction, lanes per direction (1–4), or delete it. One-way follows the displayed from → to direction.
- Mark boundary nodes **Spawn**, **Despawn**, or both. Put spawn/despawn boundaries out of view behind corners/buildings. Visibility is an authoring decision; the server has no camera-dependent spawning.
- **All roads speed limit (km/h)** sets one shared limit (3.6–90 km/h) for every road, including roads drawn afterward. Press Tab or click outside after typing to apply it live without resetting cars. Oversized entries are visibly clamped to 90 km/h. The limit is saved in each road in exported JSON; older files with mixed limits use the first road's limit for the whole network. **Apply traffic settings** also changes the target car count (0–100) and spawn interval. **Rush hour** doubles the shared speed without changing density or spawn interval; **Normal speed** restores the base speed. Straight connections between drawn segments retain the road speed limit. Simple one-way continuations and bends do not reserve a junction when only two roads meet; two-way connections still yield for opposing traffic. Free corners retain full road speed, including sharp turns. Actual intersections and queues still yield. If a selected exit remains blocked for five seconds, a waiting car tries another clear valid turn before entering; it does not change routes inside a junction. Higher density also increases spawn frequency: effective interval is `max(0.25, base interval × 20 / max(1, density))` seconds. Full lanes block spawning. Reducing density stops new entries and drains excess cars at their next node.
- **Move lanes toward curbs** measures the available asphalt width and stores per-road `laneOffset` distances, placing car centers 1.1 m inside the asphalt edge where the street permits. It includes one-way roads and can be undone. Bidirectional lanes use this distance from the centerline; additional lanes remain spaced by `laneWidth`. Two-lane one-way roads use the outer offset on both sides of the centerline; both lanes travel from → to. Single-lane one-way roads retain their selected curb: a negative offset selects the left side, positive selects the right. One-way roads without an offset use ordinary centered lane spacing. The main uphill street leaves the tram corridor open. The numeric lane-distance field allows manual adjustment; narrow roads keep ordinary lane spacing.
- **Save JSON** downloads your network; **Copy JSON** also exposes the export as selectable text if downloads are intercepted by the browser. **Load JSON** validates and applies it live. Geometry edits reset cars and reservations, then cars spawn from the edited boundaries. Density edits preserve cars. Changes are room-local until exported.

To ship an authored network, replace `packages/shared/src/traffic-network.json` with the export. The server loads this on startup and populates its road lanes immediately. This file is independent of the GLB, Blender pipeline, and terrain generation. The authored network joins the supplied strokes around Central, the uphill tram street, the campus street, and the southern streets; extend it with the editor when needed.

## Vehicle models

Each spawn independently chooses an 80% chance of a sedan, 10% sports car, or 10% farming tractor, including the initial room population. The server replicates model and color to every client; appearance stays fixed for the car's lifetime. Sedans come in gray, dark blue, white, red, or blue; sports cars in neon yellow, orange, or cyan; tractors in dark green or dark red. These are probabilities, not a fixed quota per room. Speeds, routes, and collision behavior are unchanged.

`apps/client/src/multiplayer/trafficModels.ts` builds faceted low-poly geometry with window frames, mirrors, lights, grilles, rims, and vehicle-specific details: sports-car vents/spoiler/diffuser and tractor cab/steps/exhaust/chunky unequal tires. Colored geometry is merged and instanced, with no external assets or additional render loop.

## Modules and behavior

- `packages/shared/src/traffic.ts`: JSON validation, directed right-hand lane offsets, sampled cubic intersection connectors, arc-length sampling, and conservative oriented-box overlap checks. Lane width defaults to 3.2 m. No U-turns or overtaking; matching lane indices are retained, and a narrower approach can feed either lane when the road widens. Dead ends and marked despawn nodes remove cars. Up to eight roads can meet at a node; duplicate roads between the same nodes are rejected.
- `apps/server/src/traffic.ts`: authoritative spawn timer, lane following, random valid turns, 7 m following gap, brisk acceleration (6 m/s² at normal speed), and one-car-at-a-time junction reservations. Cars check the complete turn and leave enough exit space for their following gap and rear clearance before entering, and retain their reservation until their rear clears. A reservation is released if the exit becomes blocked before entry; cars waiting five seconds may choose an alternative clear turn. Waiting cars stay behind the turning path. Oriented-box checks provide a second collision guard; small substeps prevent large jumps after server stalls. Traffic yields to parked cars and passenger transports; parked bicycles and scooters do not block it. Moving traffic cars run over active on-foot players and exposed riders (bikes, carts and Lime scooters) using the existing authoritative vehicle-hit callback, shared impact force limits, knockdown/recovery timeline, and multiplayer impact effects. Mounted riders are ejected at the impact point; both Lime occupants fall off, ownership is released, and the struck vehicle recovers after 2.5 seconds. Parked bicycles also fall over when struck, without stopping the passing car. Occupied exposed vehicles and recently struck vehicles do not stop traffic. Parked cars, bus/rail passengers, spectators, players above the car, and already knocked-down players are excluded. Contact is checked each movement substep; a player cooldown prevents repeat hits. Traffic cars do not use Rapier.
- `apps/client/src/multiplayer/traffic.ts`: nine instanced batches (paint, details, and wheels for each model); delayed position/yaw interpolation using the existing server clock. Stops at the newest received pose instead of extrapolating through obstacles. Wheels rotate with distance traveled.
- `apps/client/src/multiplayer/trafficEditor.ts`: hand drawing and click authoring, network/turn overlays, JSON IO, and live commands. It shares MapViewer's scene and frame loop. MapGame attaches it to development hosts; inspection owns a temporary room. Controls, event subscriptions, timers, meshes and rooms are cleaned up on disposal.

The editor’s `trafficRoadSurface.ts` indexes semantic `G_Road` triangles for local cross-street centering and height lookup; `trafficEditing.ts` converts strokes into independent nodes/roads. The editor uses this lookup for authoring; car rendering reuses it to sit on the asphalt and follow its slope. Missing asphalt or a different bridge deck falls back to the authored height. Server routing and collision logic remain independent of city geometry. It does not rebuild or alter city geometry. The network owns road elevations; generated lanes interpolate the authored Y values. Author enough nodes over hills and bends to follow the actual street surface, and keep roads/turns outside buildings. No full-city automatic road extraction, geometry collision routing, traffic lights, overtaking, player-car rigid-body collisions, or visibility culling is included. A fully blocked cyclic network may gridlock; use sensible boundary exits and density for its capacity. All clients receive the car state; new maps target 60 cars, with an authoritative cap of 60 cars per room.

Validation: `npm run check`, `npm run lint`, `npm test`, and `npm run build -w @race/client`. `tests/traffic-system.test.ts` covers directed lanes, turns, intersection throughput/non-overlap, blocked spawning, density, JSON replacement, and real two-client Colyseus replication/authorization; renderer tests cover interpolation and cleanup. Authoring tests cover asphalt centering/height, junction connections, endpoint flags and reversible deletion.
